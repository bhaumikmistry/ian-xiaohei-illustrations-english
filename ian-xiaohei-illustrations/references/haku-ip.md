# Haku IP

## Character Definition

Haku is a secondary visual IP for inline article illustrations, inspired by the river spirit aesthetic.

Haku is a silent, mysterious presence — not a ghost meant to scare, but a quiet observer-participant who drifts through systems and structures. Unlike Xiaohei (who is a hands-on worker), Haku is the one who *witnesses*, *channels*, *absorbs*, and *passes through* — making it ideal for themes around flow, transition, hidden processes, and intangible forces.

## Appearance

- Tall, narrow black body — like a void cloak or dark column with no visible edges.
- White oval mask face, centered on the upper body.
- Two simple dark oval eyes on the mask — no pupils, slightly elongated vertically.
- A small, minimal mouth mark — not expressive, just present.
- Grey/purple tear-like marks running down from under each eye.
- No legs — the dark robe/body fades away at the bottom, giving a floating/hovering presence.
- Visible hands — simple, dark, extending from the robe. Always present, not hidden.
- Hand-drawn with slightly uneven outlines — not too clean, not too rough.
- Scale: taller and thinner than Xiaohei. Should feel like it looms gently, not aggressively.

## Personality

- Silent and observant — communicates through presence, not speech.
- Mysterious but not threatening.
- Moves through systems like water moves through pipes — naturally, without force.
- Patient. Waits at thresholds, gates, and transition points.
- Absorbs and channels — takes things in on one side, releases them on the other.
- Slightly melancholic but functional — sadness serves the metaphor, not the mood.

## Common Duties

Have Haku participate in the core action through presence and flow:

- Standing at a threshold or gate between two states.
- Absorbing inputs into its void body — things enter, transformed things exit.
- Hovering beside a flow, hands outstretched to channel its direction.
- Sitting inside a container (well, box, room) as the hidden process.
- Mask facing the viewer while the body acts as a conduit or filter.
- Drifting along a path, marking the transition from one phase to another.
- Hands reaching out to hold, pass, or block something at a decision point.
- Being the "black box" — input goes in one side of Haku, output comes out the other.
- Watching over a system quietly while something transforms around it.
- Splitting into two: mask stays still, body extends to connect two separate elements.

## Prohibitions

- Don't make Haku scary, horror-style, or threatening.
- Don't add detail to the mask — keep it minimal (eyes, mouth mark, tear marks only).
- Don't give Haku legs or feet — the robe fades away at the bottom, always floating.
- Don't make Haku too active/energetic — it should feel still and deliberate, not frantic.
- Don't use Haku as a decorative ghost in the background.
- Don't add extra facial features, blush, or emoji expressions.
- Don't draw Haku with complex patterns, textures, or clothing details on the body.
- Don't make it too small — Haku should have presence and height in the composition.

## Best Used For

Haku works especially well for these themes (prefer Haku over Xiaohei when the article is about):

- Hidden processes, black boxes, "what happens inside"
- Flow, transition, transformation, passing through stages
- Intangible forces: trust, attention, energy, time
- Thresholds and gates: decisions, filters, access control
- Absorption and release: learning, digestion, synthesis
- Quiet accumulation: compounding, patience, slow growth

## Quality Standard

If removing Haku still leaves the image's core metaphor fully intact, then Haku is too decorative — rewrite the prompt to make Haku the conduit, threshold, or transformation agent.

Haku's test is different from Xiaohei's: Xiaohei should be *doing* the action. Haku should be *embodying* the process — it IS the filter, the gate, the channel, not just operating one.
